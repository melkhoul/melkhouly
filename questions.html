AI99 · AI-enabled workflow transformation

Learning-development requests were taking an average of 7.5 months. The visible symptom was capacity, but the larger problem was a workflow that could not respond quickly enough to performance gaps and changing requirements. Rather than treating additional resources as the only answer, I influenced TD leadership to establish a reduction goal, negotiated the formation of the team’s first scrum team and served as Product Manager. We compared four internal AI options and three external vendors, selected an approach, ran two UAT rounds with 23 L&D leaders, and built an adoption mechanism around the product.

Foundational-content design time fell by 99% and overall soft-skills turnaround fell by 60%. A 298-member community and 1,755 views within eight months gave us additional evidence of reach, although views should not be confused with unique adoption. What stayed with me was that AI creates value only when the workflow, roles, adoption mechanism and measures around the technology change with it.

Experimentation in L&D · evidence before preference

Earlier in my career, I became increasingly uncomfortable with a common assumption in learning: that a more elaborate intervention must be a better one. I started treating design choices as hypotheses rather than preferences. My experimentation approach defined the hypothesis, success metric, monitoring metric and do-no-harm measure before the intervention, then forced an explicit go, no-go or inconclusive decision.

In one test, interactive eLearning generated only a 1% improvement in satisfaction while requiring 340% more development time. In another, a screencast-based approach produced 37% higher retention than ILT in the tested context. The broader lesson became part of how I make decisions today: evidence must be allowed to stop an attractive idea, not merely validate it.

Learning Strategy Builder · evidence-informed strategy

As my scope expanded, individual learning solutions stopped being the right unit of analysis. The harder question became how a function makes coherent choices across populations, programs, technology, people, governance and investment. I developed the Learning Strategy Builder to connect three drivers—organizational business goals, annual needs analysis and content maintenance—to nine components: population, programs, metrics, staffing, technology, capabilities, outsourcing, governance and budget.

The intention was not to create another planning template. It was to give senior learning leaders a mechanism for translating business priorities into a multi-year capability strategy and making the choices visible enough to align stakeholders around them.

HirePulse · decision intelligence

New-hire onboarding was extending beyond three months, while performance across training, OJT and live production was difficult to diagnose early enough to act. I established a measurement structure across knowledge, quality, productivity and engagement, mapped ownership and cadence, conducted root-cause analysis and developed an AI-enabled mechanism to surface patterns and risk earlier.

The work forecasted graduation timing that supported a 16% reduction in training duration—four of 25 days—and flagged four of the eventual five lowest performers after one week compared with the later nine-week result. The experience changed how I think about analytics: the value is not the dashboard or model itself. The value is whether the organization can make a better decision early enough to change the outcome.

HirePro · challenging assumptions with evidence

An assessment had already been used with 13,000 applicants, with a stated investment basis of INR455K. Instead of assuming that an established mechanism was working because it was already embedded, I led a validation study with Talent Acquisition and Operations. We linked assessment, graduation and on-job performance data and used correlation and regression analysis on 176 complete records.

The analysis found no significant relationship between assessment scores and actual performance. I recommended reevaluating the approach and drafted a role-specific alternative for further development. I value this example because the output was not a new product or a positive result. It was evidence that challenged an existing investment.

Career Resilience to AI · portfolio strategy under uncertainty

During 2026 planning, Career Choice needed a more evidence-based way to consider how AI could reshape the durability of different career pathways. There was no perfect dataset capable of answering that question. I developed and tested an AI-supported research methodology, validated outputs across multiple AI tools and used a High, Medium and Low AI-resilience lens to inform maintain, enhance or deactivate decisions.

The analysis informed portfolio choices including discontinuation of Corporate Roles careers, while country/career offerings were reduced by 23%, from 168 to 130. The important capability was not prompt engineering. It was building a sufficiently robust decision mechanism under uncertainty and using it to make portfolio trade-offs.

AI Adoption · from access to behavior

Tool availability is only one condition for adoption. I started by measuring actual usage and barriers rather than assuming that more training was the answer. The intervention combined tailored resources, seven knowledge-sharing sessions over 2.5 months, peer examples and collaborative work on real pain points. Daily AI use moved from 48% to 68%, while reported non-use fell from four people to zero among 22 post-initiative respondents. I treat that as evidence of an early adoption intervention, not proof of enterprise-wide transformation.

The experience sharpened a broader distinction in my thinking. Adoption asks, “How can AI help us do what we already do?” Transformation asks, “What should we do differently because AI exists?” My current approach therefore looks beyond learning to Adoption, Infrastructure, Governance, Leadership and Capability—and connects capability to adoption, behavior and workflow change, and ultimately business outcomes.
