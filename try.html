Frameworks

Mechanisms for making better capability decisions.

These frameworks capture ideas I have developed across learning, capability, evidence and AI. Each is intended to make a recurring organizational decision more explicit, evidence-informed and scalable.

Transformation framework

AI Adoption & Transformation

AI adoption is not AI transformation. Adoption asks how AI can improve existing work. Transformation asks what the organization should do differently because AI exists.

The framework looks across five organizational conditions: Adoption, Infrastructure, Governance, Leadership and Capability.

It connects those conditions to behavior and workflow change, and ultimately to business outcomes: Capability → Adoption → Behavior/workflow change → Business impact.

This framework will continue to evolve as additional transformation evidence is incorporated.

Strategy framework

Learning Strategy Builder

Capability strategy requires choices, not a catalogue of learning activity. The framework connects business priorities to the organizational components required to execute them.

Three drivers: business goals, annual needs analysis and content maintenance.

Nine components: population, programs, metrics, staffing, technology, capabilities, outsourcing, governance and budget.

Its purpose is to make trade-offs visible and translate business priorities into a coherent multi-year capability strategy.

Decision framework

Experimentation in L&D

Evidence should be able to stop an attractive idea. Design choices are treated as hypotheses rather than preferences.

Define the hypothesis, success metric, monitoring metric, do-no-harm measure and explicit decision rule before running the intervention.

The purpose is disciplined decision-making: evidence can validate an idea, challenge it—or stop it.

Design framework

eLearning Design Map

Start with the performance problem, not the learning asset. The map connects diagnosis, design, implementation and outcome measurement.

Data Collection → Validation → Proposal → Project → Impact → Content → Design → Development → Implementation → Outcome.

It reflects an earlier stage of my work, but the principle remains central: design decisions should be grounded in context, constraints, root cause and measurable outcomes.
